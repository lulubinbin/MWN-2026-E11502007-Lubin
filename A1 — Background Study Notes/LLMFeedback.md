Low text density. Most slides carry a short headline plus one sub-line — you're clearly practicing "keep it simple" rather than just stating it.

### Where it could be stronger

One slide breaks your own "consistent design" rule. The "My Project Proposal" slide switches to a flat light-blue box on a plain background, which stands out against the gradient look used everywhere else. Since "use consistent design" is literally tip #5, this is the one place a viewer might notice the mismatch — worth restyling to match the rest of the deck.
The "use images" and "keep data simple" tips aren't carried into your own content. The skyline photo demonstrates tip #3 well, but the later slides (the three reading passes, the project proposal) are text-only. A small diagram, icon, or simple chart on the project proposal slide (e.g., illustrating "basic path" or "effect of distance on QoS") would let you practice tip #3 and #4 on real content, not just the example slide.
Bullet slides could use a touch more visual hierarchy. "My Project Proposal" uses plain bullets for two sub-topics; a slightly bolder distinction (icons, two-column layout, or a small visual per bullet) would make it easier to scan at a glance.
Pacing on repeated slides. A couple of slides (e.g., "Third Pass") appear to hold for a while with no new visual change — if that reflects a long verbal explanation, consider adding a supporting visual or breaking the point across two slides so the screen keeps pace with what's being said.
