## How to read a paper

### General informations

**Author**: S. Keshav (and students)  
**School**: University of Waterloo, Ontario, Canada  
  
The paper aims to provide a method for efficient and productive reading of academic papers.  
--> Centered around the **3 pass approach**

### The first pass : a bird's-eye view
Key steps:
- Read the title, abstract, introduction
- Read the table of contents (titles, section and subsections headings, ...)
- Read the conclusions 
- and lastly Read the references, looking for anything you maight have read. 
  
After this you should be able to answer the 5 Cs: Category, context, correctness, contributions, and clarity. 

- Duration: a few minutes

### The second pass : understand most of the paper
Read the paper again with more care looking at everything but not trying to go too much into details (skip proofs for instance). 

- Duration: ~1 hour

### The third pass : mentally re-implement the paper
By trying to mentally recreate the paper, you may learn more about the way the author think. 
  
In this step you should also go into detail (and read all the proofs)

- Duration: ~1 hour if experienced (but 4 or 5 for everyone else)

### Other insights
- You don't have to do the 3 steps, you may chose to stop at each point. 
- You may want to do a litterature survey (reading a lot of linked papers) to train your paper reading skills and to learn about a scientific field.
